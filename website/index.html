Features

Built to explain, not just to alarm

PhishGuard runs inside Gmail and shows its reasoning, so you can decide with the evidence in front of you.

Phishing signal detection

Flags urgent or threatening language, requests for passwords, payment or verification codes, link shorteners, punycode, raw IP addresses, brand-lookalike domains, and links that don't match the sender.

Inbox context

Compares the open email with the recent messages listed in your Gmail inbox: a brand claimed from the wrong domain, a sender you've never heard from, or a delivery or invoice with no matching order.

Explainable risk scores

A compact badge on the email expands into a 0–100 score, a plain-language verdict, the signals grouped by category, and a recommended action.

Optional AI review

When you opt in, emails with an uncertain local score get a Gemini review through a PhishGuard server you run. Clear-cut cases are decided locally.

Privacy-conscious design

The Gemini API key stays on the server, never in the extension. Email content is only sent for AI review after you turn it on, and your other inbox emails are never sent.

Graceful fallback

If AI review is off, not needed, times out or fails, you still get the full local result, and the panel says which one happened.

How it works

Three steps, inside the inbox you already use

  1. 1

    Open an email in Gmail

    PhishGuard checks the open message automatically with its local rules and your inbox context. A small badge appears next to the subject.

  2. 2

    Review the score and signals

    Select the badge to see the risk score, the verdict and each warning sign it found, grouped so you can scan them quickly.

  3. 3

    Decide: trust, verify or avoid

    Follow the recommended action. When in doubt, contact the sender through a channel you already trust, not the links in the email.

Where AI fits in

AI review is off by default. If you turn it on, PhishGuard asks Gemini only when the local score falls in the uncertain middle range (20–59). Low-risk and high-risk emails are decided locally, and nothing is sent while the setting is off.

Security & privacy

Clear about what stays and what's sent

Here's exactly how PhishGuard handles your email, based on how the extension is built today.

Local by default

Rules and inbox-context checks run in your browser. With AI review off, no email content leaves your device.

AI review is opt-in

When enabled and needed, the email's subject, sender, body (up to 12,000 characters), up to 50 links, and PhishGuard's short findings go to your PhishGuard server, which forwards them to Google Gemini. Your other inbox emails are never sent.

The API key stays on the server

The Gemini key lives in the server's configuration. The extension never sees it and only talks to the local server you run.

Estimates, not guarantees

A score is a heuristic estimate. A low score doesn't prove an email is safe, and a high one doesn't prove it's malicious. Always verify unexpected requests independently.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly

What is PhishGuard?

A Chrome extension that checks the email you open in Gmail for common phishing signs and shows a risk score with the reasons behind it. It combines local detection rules, context from your inbox and, if you choose, an AI review by Google Gemini.

Does it work with Gmail?

Yes. It currently works in Gmail on the web (mail.google.com) in Chrome. Other email providers and the Gmail mobile apps aren't supported.

Does it use AI on every email?

No. AI review is off by default. If you turn it on, PhishGuard only asks Gemini about emails whose local score is uncertain (20–59). Emails the local checks score as clearly low or clearly high risk aren't sent.

What email data is shared when AI analysis is enabled?

For an email that needs AI review: its subject, sender, body (up to 12,000 characters), up to 50 links, and PhishGuard's own short findings about it. These go to the PhishGuard server running on your computer, which forwards them to Google Gemini under Google's API terms. Your other inbox emails are never sent. Turning AI review off stops further requests.

Can it guarantee that an email is safe?

No. PhishGuard gives an estimate based on the signals it can see. It can miss phishing emails and can flag legitimate ones. Treat its score as one input, and verify unexpected requests for money, passwords or personal details through a channel you already trust.

How do I install it?

PhishGuard isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet. For now you can load it as a developer preview: get the source from GitHub, open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, and choose Load unpacked. See Install PhishGuard for the steps.
